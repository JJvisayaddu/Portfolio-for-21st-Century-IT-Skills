        <code>─── 🎧 EXPLANATION ───</code>
      </div>
      <br>
      <p align="justify">
        In this activity, I applied the guidelines from our personal branding lesson to design a cohesive branding kit for my chosen product, establishing a distinct visual identity that sets it apart and attracts its target audience.
      </p>
      <hr>
      <p align="left"><strong>What:</strong> A complete brand identity kit featuring a logo, tagline, header, color, and typography guide, and a resume header for an ube-based product.</p>
      <p align="left"><strong>Why:</strong> To promote and elevate local Filipino food, <em>ube (Dioscorea alata)</em>, to the same popularity as mainstream coffee or matcha shops through a fun brand named Ubelala.</p>
      <p align="left"><strong>How:</strong></p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Color Basis:</strong> Used the graphic image of the ube tuber in the logo as the main reference for the brand's palette.</li>
        <li><strong>Color Adjustments:</strong> Adjusted the tone, shade, and tint of the ube colors to create a sweet purple and pink palette representing its natural look.</li>
        <li><strong>Brand Symbolism:</strong> Paired custom ube and root graphics to symbolize authentic, farm-grown ingredients and keep the brand grounded in local heritage.</li>
        <li><strong>Template &amp; Font Exploration:</strong> Explored multiple design templates to study logo structures and typography, then modified them with custom ideas to fit the ube product identity.</li>
      </ul>
      <br>

### 🎧 TRACK.STORY // DESCRIPTION 🎧

<p align="justify">
I decided to build this branding kit around <em>ube (Dioscorea alata)</em>, a well-known Filipino favorite. Although the Philippines leads in producing and exporting <em>ube</em>, there are very few shops dedicated entirely to it compared to coffee or matcha. I chose <em>ube</em> because it is one of my favorite foods, and I want to create a strong visual identity that elevates it to a popular trend.
</p>

<br>

<table>
  <tr>
    <td width="33%" align="left" valign="top">
      <br>
      <div align="center"><code>◄◄ UBELALA ►►</code></div>
      <br>
      <p align="left">➤ <strong>Ubelala</strong></p>
      <ul>
        <li>I deliberately chose Ubelala over the expected name <em>ubelaya</em> to give the brand a deeper, more intentional meaning.</li>
        <li>"Lala" highlights how the sweetness of ube can instantly cheer you up and brighten your mood.</li>
        <li>It also plays on the Filipino word <em>alala</em> ("remember"), asking "<em>naalala mo pa ba ang ube?</em>" ("do you still remember ube?").</li>
        <li>I chose this because people often favor imported trends over local foods, and I want to remind everyone to celebrate and enjoy our own Filipino products like Ube.</li>
      </ul>
      <br>
    </td>
    <td width="33%" align="left" valign="top">
      <br>
      <div align="center"><code>◄◄ TAGLINE ►►</code></div>
      <br>
      <p align="left">➤ <strong>Life is better in Purple and Sweet</strong></p>
      <ul>
        <li>This tagline highlights the product's core identity through color and taste.</li>
        <li>"Purple" represents the iconic color of ube, while "Sweet" refers to its comforting, delicious flavor.</li>
        <li>Together, it conveys the idea that enjoying a sweet, purple ube treat makes everyday life happier and more enjoyable.</li>
      </ul>
      <br>
    </td>
    <td width="33%" align="left" valign="top">
      <br>
      <div align="center"><code>◄◄ ROOTS (HEADER) ►►</code></div>
      <br>
      <p align="left">➤ <strong>Roots (header)</strong></p>
      <ul>
        <li>The root graphics represent <em>ube</em> as a natural root crop and tuber.</li>
        <li>Visually, the roots symbolize authenticity, highlighting that the brand uses real, farm-grown Filipino ube rather than artificial flavorings.</li>
        <li>They also serve as a subtle visual reminder to stay grounded in our local culture while helping ube grow into a trendy, mainstream product.</li>
      </ul>
      <br>
    </td>
  </tr>
</table>
